Layton vs Armaf Derby: Is the $32 Dupe Worth It?

Dupes & Comparisons  ·  November 2, 2026  ·  5 min read

Layton from Parfums de Marly is one of the most talked-about "smells expensive" fragrances in the niche world, and at $250 a bottle, people go looking for a cheaper way in almost immediately. The name that comes up most often is Armaf Derby, which sells for $32, a little over a tenth of the price. We lined them up note for note using what's in our catalog to see how much of Layton actually makes it into the budget version.

That price gap is close to eightfold. The question worth asking isn't whether Derby is identical to Layton, it's whether it keeps the parts of Layton that made people want it in the first place.

What Layton is made of

Layton was created by Hamid Merati Kashani and released in 2016. Our catalog lists its top notes as apple, bergamot, and lavender, a heart of geranium and jasmine, and a base of vanilla, cardamom, and sandalwood. It carries Amber, Fresh Spicy, and Sweet accords, holds a 4.5 rating across 1,680 reviews, and is tagged very long lasting (12 hours or more) with strong projection. The combination of sweet apple up top and warm vanilla underneath is the part most people describe when they talk about Layton.

What Armaf Derby keeps, and what it drops

Derby's note list in our catalog is shorter than Layton's, which is typical of fragrances built to echo a bigger name at a fraction of the cost. It shares apple and bergamot up top with Layton, keeps geranium in the heart, and holds on to vanilla and sandalwood underneath. What it leaves out is lavender from the opening, jasmine from the heart, and cardamom from the base, the notes that give Layton some of its spice and florancy.

That overlap, apple and bergamot opening into a vanilla and sandalwood base, is enough to land in the same general neighborhood as Layton. It will not fool anyone who has worn the original side by side, but it is clearly built around the same sweet-apple-and-amber idea rather than being an unrelated scent with a similar bottle.

Where the gap actually shows up

The biggest difference in our catalog isn't the notes, it's the performance. Layton is tagged very long lasting at 12 hours or more with strong projection. Derby is tagged long lasting at 7 to 12 hours with moderate projection. That's still a solid, all-day fragrance for $32, just not the room-filling presence Layton is known for. Derby's 4.1 rating across 3,960 reviews is also respectable, though it sits below Layton's 4.5 across 1,680 reviews.

The verdict

Derby is not a clone of Layton, and it isn't trying to pass itself off as one. It keeps the part of Layton's formula that most people are actually chasing, the sweet apple opening resolving into vanilla and sandalwood, and drops the lavender, jasmine, and cardamom that gave the original some of its spice and complexity. The tradeoff is fair for the price: strong projection becomes moderate, and 12 plus hours becomes 7 to 12.

If what draws you to Layton is that specific sweet-amber idea and you want to try it before committing $250, Derby is a reasonable $32 way in. If what you love about Layton is how long it lasts and how far it projects, that's the part Derby doesn't fully replicate, and no amount of reapplying quite closes that gap.
