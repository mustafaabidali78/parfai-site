Why weather changes what you should wear

Heat and humidity amplify projection — a heavy amber or gourmand that's cozy in December can turn cloying and overwhelming at 35°C, while light citrus and aquatic notes stay pleasant and perform exactly as intended. Cold does the opposite: it suppresses how far a scent travels, so the same light citrus that shines in summer barely registers in winter, while dense woody, spicy and amber compositions finally get to show their full sillage. This isn't a gimmick — perfumers plan around this, and enthusiasts already rotate their collection by season by hand. This feature just does that rotation automatically, every day, using the actual conditions outside.

How this works

Locating you, worldwide, without asking first. The pick above starts from an IP-based location lookup — no permission prompt, resolves instantly — so it works the moment you land on the page, anywhere in the world. If you want it more precise, "Use precise location" asks your browser directly for GPS-level coordinates. Nothing about your exact location is ever stored beyond the resolved city, country, and that day's weather snapshot, cached in your browser only, for a few hours.

Live, not simulated. Current temperature and conditions come from Open-Meteo, a free weather API, fetched fresh for your coordinates every time the cache expires. If your network blocks the lookup or it fails for any reason, you get a manual city picker instead of a broken or fake result — pick a city and it pulls real live weather for that city instead.

Matching weather to fragrance, no AI call needed. Temperature and conditions sort into a handful of bands — hot, mild, cold, rainy, snowy — and each band maps to a small set of target accord families, the same family data already on every product page (Fresh and Aromatic for hot, Woody and Amber and Oriental for cold, Fresh and Chypre for rain). The catalog is filtered and ranked by family match plus rating, and the top result becomes today's pick, with two alternates offered alongside it.

Locale-correct by default. Temperature displays in °C or °F automatically based on your resolved country.
