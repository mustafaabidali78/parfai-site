Weather-Aware Pick of the Day

Every morning, ParfAI would look at the real live weather wherever you actually are — not a guess, not a hardcoded region — and recommend a fragrance from the catalog suited to how it performs in heat, cold, humidity or rain.

Why weather changes what you should wear

Heat and humidity amplify projection — a heavy amber or gourmand that's cozy in December can turn cloying and overwhelming at 35°C, while light citrus and aquatic notes stay pleasant and perform exactly as intended. Cold does the opposite: it suppresses how far a scent travels, so the same light citrus that shines in summer barely registers in winter, while dense woody, spicy and amber compositions finally get to show their full sillage. This isn't a gimmick — perfumers plan around this, and enthusiasts already rotate their collection by season by hand. This feature would just do that rotation automatically, every day, using the actual conditions outside.

Works anywhere — not just one climate

The pick would be computed from wherever you're actually logging in from, worldwide — a visitor in Dubai and a visitor in Oslo get completely different recommendations on the same day, because they're feeling completely different weather. How it would actually work

Getting your location, worldwide, with consent. The page would ask your browser for permission to use your location. If you say yes, that resolves to a city and country and a live weather API call runs against it. If you say no, or your browser doesn't support it, it falls back to a coarser IP-based location — clearly labeled as approximate — with a "not your city?" link to search and set it manually. Nothing about your exact coordinates would ever be stored; only the resolved city, country, and the day's weather snapshot, and only long enough to compute and cache that day's pick.

Live, not simulated. A weather API (a free option like Open-Meteo needs no API key and no cost, which keeps this cheap to run at scale) would supply temperature, humidity, and condition for that city in real time. It refreshes at most once or twice a day per visitor — weather doesn't change fast enough to justify calling it on every page load, and caching keeps the feature fast and within free-tier limits.

Matching weather to fragrance, no AI call needed. Temperature and humidity sort into simple bands — hot/dry, hot/humid, mild, cold/dry, cold/wet — and each band maps to a small set of target accords, the same accord data already on every product page (Fresh and Citrus for hot, Woody and Amber and Warm Spicy for cold, Green for rain). The catalog gets filtered and ranked by accord match plus rating, and the top result becomes the day's pick, with one or two alternates offered underneath.

Locale-correct by default. Temperature would display in °C or °F automatically based on the visitor's resolved country, with a manual toggle for anyone who prefers the other unit regardless of where they are.
