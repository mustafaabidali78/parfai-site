Fragrance News Roundup: November 2026

Fragrance News  ·  November 23, 2026  ·  6 min read

Three stories are worth knowing about this month. A major fragrance company is narrowing its focus around a handful of brands, a French niche house is testing how far shoppers will go to buy a scent sight unseen, and celebrity-fronted fragrances are having their biggest year in a long time. Here is what is actually happening, as of November 23, 2026, with the sources behind each story at the bottom.

Coty narrows its focus, and Marc Jacobs comes out on top

On November 11, 2026, Personal Care Insights reported that Coty made three moves in a single week that point toward a smaller, more focused company. The clearest winner is Marc Jacobs. Coty extended its license with WHP Global and G-III Apparel Group, the brand's co-owners, for forty years, a partnership that goes back to 2003. Daisy Marc Jacobs, Daisy Wild, and Perfect Marc Jacobs are now ranked among the top ten to fifteen prestige women's fragrances in the US, UK, Canada, and Australia, according to Coty's own figures, and the brand's relaunched makeup line is running ahead of target at Sephora in the US, UK, and Australia and New Zealand.

The second move runs the other way. Coty agreed back in July to hand the Gucci Beauty license back to Kering a year early, with L'Oréal taking over from July 1, 2027. Coty will keep running Gucci Beauty until at least June 30, 2027, and will be paid around four hundred million US dollars across two installments for stepping back early.

The third move is the hardest on the people involved. Unions told the French news agency AFP that Coty plans to cut 155 of its roughly 200 jobs in France, split between head office roles in Paris and field sales staff. Coty has not confirmed that figure, saying only that its French proposal covers a restructuring of mass-market beauty and a revision of how it runs its prestige business, with further talks scheduled for late November. Unions suspect the cuts would make it easier to eventually sell off mass-market brands such as Bourjois and Rimmel, something Coty has not confirmed. The cuts follow a global restructuring announced in April 2025 that already targeted around 700 roles, and they land as Coty's six biggest prestige brands, including Marc Jacobs, Burberry, and Calvin Klein, turned a sales decline into growth last quarter.

A $775 perfume you are not allowed to smell first

French house Floraïku Paris is adding four new scents to its ultra-luxury Edition 3776 collection before the holidays, Glossy reported on October 20, 2026: Make My Heart Sing, Many Different Stories, Lost For Words, and Bows And Arrows, joining I Am Not A Flower, which launched back in 2018. Each bottle costs $775, roughly double the brand's standard $395 fragrances, and the whole collection is sold only online in the US. That means buying a full bottle without smelling it first, something Floraïku's other scents do not ask of shoppers, since those are sold in stores such as The Scent Room in Los Angeles.

Co-founder John Molloy's reasoning is simple: the brand wanted to use the best possible ingredients without being limited by what a bottle needs to cost on a store shelf. "There's no barrier," he told Glossy, adding that he is confident the online store can deliver the white-glove experience a $775 purchase calls for.

The timing lines up with wider numbers in the same article. Citing Circana data, Glossy reported that US prestige fragrance prices rose 5 percent in the first half of 2026 while the number of bottles sold stayed flat, and that luxury fragrance sales grew in both dollars and units over the same period. At the same time, the department stores that have traditionally sold luxury scent are shrinking, Neiman Marcus closed its Dallas flagship in September among them. A brand betting its most loyal fans will buy sight unseen is, in part, a bet that those stores are not coming back.

Celebrity fragrances are back in a big way

Now Smell This described 2026 as a standout year for celebrity-fronted fragrance launches in a November 13, 2026 post. Kylie Jenner expanded her Cosmic line with a Mood Stone trio. Ice Spice entered the fragrance business with In Ha Mood, a partnership with Revlon. Megan Thee Stallion launched Hot Girl Summer, a limited-edition unisex scent made with Coty. And singer Tate McRae confirmed that her long-rumored beauty project is, in fact, an eponymous fragrance brand.

McRae's fragrance, Tate 01, launched in October through a partnership with Scent Beauty, developed by Givaudan perfumers Adriana Medina and Antoine Maisondieu around what the brand calls a second-skin effect, where the scent is meant to shift slightly with each wearer's own skin chemistry. It opens with bergamot and clary sage, moves into a white floral heart, and settles into amber, tonka bean, and sandalwood. The campaign, photographed by Mario Sorrenti in black and white, leans into a '90s supermodel look, and the fragrance sells for $30 to $82 through tatebytm.com, tied to McRae's Miss Possessive Tour and her third album, So Close To What.

What this means if you're just trying to find your next scent

None of this changes what actually smells good on you, but it says something about where the industry's attention is right now. A conglomerate the size of Coty is choosing to go deeper on fewer brands, a niche house is betting its most loyal customers will buy blind, and a new wave of musicians are putting their names on fragrance much like the 2000s did with Britney Spears and Jennifer Lopez. If a celebrity name or a house you follow keeps showing up in stories like these, our celebrity perfumes page and full catalogue are worth a look once those fragrances reach shelves and real reviews.
