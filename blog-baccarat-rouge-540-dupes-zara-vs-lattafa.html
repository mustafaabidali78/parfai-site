Baccarat Rouge 540 Dupes Compared: Zara Red Vanilla vs Lattafa Ameer Al Oudh

It lists at $325 in our catalog, so it's no surprise that two budget names come up whenever someone asks for the same idea for less: Zara Red Vanilla at $26 and Lattafa Ameer Al Oudh at $28. We lined all three up, note for note, to see what each alternative keeps and what it leaves out.

Both alternatives cost less than a tenth of the original. The real question is how much of Baccarat Rouge's character comes along for the ride.

What Baccarat Rouge 540 is made of

Created by Francis Kurkdjian and released in 2015, Baccarat Rouge 540 opens with saffron and jasmine, moves into amberwood and ambergris, and settles on fir resin, cedar, and musk. Our catalog tags it with Amber, Sweet, Woody, and Floral accords, rates it 4.5 across 2,840 reviews, and lists it as very long lasting (12 hours or more) with strong projection. The saffron and amberwood pairing is the profile it's best known for.

Zara Red Vanilla: the closest match on paper

On the note list alone, Red Vanilla is the nearer of the two. It shares four of Baccarat Rouge's notes: saffron up top, jasmine and amberwood in the heart, and musk underneath. What it drops are the ambergris, fir resin, and cedar that give the original its woody backbone, and what it adds is vanilla, which pushes the whole thing in a sweeter, softer direction.

The trade-off is staying power. Our catalog lists Red Vanilla as moderate in both longevity (4 to 7 hours) and projection, against 12 hours or more and strong projection for the original. It holds a 4.0 rating across 2,760 reviews, which is respectable for a $26 bottle, but expect to reapply during a long day.

Lattafa Ameer Al Oudh: warmer, with oud

Ameer Al Oudh takes a different route. It shares saffron and musk with Baccarat Rouge, but swaps the jasmine and amberwood for oud and rose, and builds its base on amber rather than fir resin and cedar. Think of it as a relative rather than a twin: still a warm, saffron-led amber, but with more oud presence and a woodier, deeper feel.

The verdict

Neither of these is a clone, and neither is trying to be. Which one suits you depends on what you love most about Baccarat Rouge 540.

If it's the saffron, jasmine, and amberwood combination, Red Vanilla is the nearer match and the sweeter, softer take, at the cost of lasting roughly 4 to 7 hours. If it's the presence, meaning a fragrance that lasts and fills a room, Ameer Al Oudh gets closer on performance, but it earns that with a warmer, oud-leaning character that is its own thing rather than a copy.

What neither alternative reproduces is the original's fir resin, cedar, and ambergris base, or its 12-hour-plus staying power. If those are the parts you're after, the original is still the original. At $26 and $28, though, both are low-risk ways to find out how much of the idea you actually need.
